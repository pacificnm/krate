# Klang: brief

2026-10-30

Klang is a sampler for the [Krate](../../../README.md) suite: 16 pads, each playing a WAV or MP3 file of your own (or a slice of one) as a one-shot, a held note or a loop locked to the tempo, through its own filter, drive, bitcrusher and ring modulator, with a trance gate, delay and reverb; a pattern sequencer with accents, stutters, reverse and pitch per step; slicing a loop across the pads. It is built for the cut-up, mechanical, gated sound of industrial and trance: drum breaks chopped and re-sequenced, voices reversed and crushed, drones chopped by the gate. It runs standalone (`klang`) and as a device in the Krate rack.

It follows the classic late-80s groove samplers in spirit: a grid of pads, a sample at a time to edit, patterns of steps. Every name, logo and graphic is our own: the panel reads "Klang" and "Sampler". No samples ship with it: you load your own (the starter kit is generated by Klang itself).

Related docs: [MANUAL.md](MANUAL.md) (the player's manual), [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). Suite design: [SHARED_FOUNDATIONS.MD](../../../docs/SHARED_FOUNDATIONS.MD).

## Scope (v1)

- **16 pads**, each one voice (a new hit restarts it, with a 2 ms fade so it doesn't click), stereo. A pad plays a region (START … END) of a sample, at its pitch (TUNE, FINE), shaped by an attack and a decay, in one of three **modes**: **One shot** (plays to the end or the decay, whatever the key does), **Gate** (plays while the key or the step is held), **Loop** (loops the region until the pad is stopped: its key let go, another hit, its choke group, or the transport stopping). **SYNC** locks a loop (or any region) to 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 beats of the tempo by playing it faster or slower (the pitch follows, as on the old samplers); TUNE then plays on top.
- **Per pad**: REVERSE; a filter (**Off**, low-pass, high-pass, band-pass) with cutoff, resonance and an envelope sweep; **DRIVE** (soft clip to fold-over); a **bitcrusher** (bits 16 … 2, and the rate down to 1/32); a **ring modulator** (amount and frequency); **GATE** (the trance gate chops it); a **choke group** (1–4: a pad cuts the others in its group, as an open hi-hat by a closed one); sends to the delay and the reverb; level and pan.
- **Trance gate**: a 16-step on/off pattern (each pattern its own) at 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32, with depth and smoothing, chopping the pads that have GATE on.
- **Effects**: tempo-synced ping-pong delay and reverb, fed by each pad's sends.
- **Patterns**: 16 patterns (A–P), each up to 64 steps of 1/16 (four pages of 16), with swing. A step on a pad: off or on, **accent**, **repeat** (1, 2, 3 or 4 hits in the step: rolls and stutters), **reverse**, **pitch** (−12 … +12 semitones). Patterns run on the transport; a new pattern starts at the end of the current pass.
- **Slicing**: a pad's sample cut into 4, 8 or 16 equal slices, or at its hits (transients), onto that pad and the ones after it (each slice a pad with the same sample and its own START and END).
- **Samples**: WAV (16/24/32-bit, float) or MP3, mono or stereo, any rate, loaded with a file dialog or dropped on a pad, copied into Klang's sample library (`~/.local/share/klang/samples`).
- **Playing**: MIDI notes 36–51 (C2–D#3, the usual pad notes) are pads 1–16, with velocity; with **KEYS** on Chromatic, every key plays the edited pad at its pitch (C4 as recorded). Clicking a pad plays it and selects it for editing.
- **MIDI**: notes, Program Change selects a pattern, MIDI Learn for every control and button; in the rack, default channel 11.

## Sound engine

- **Samples** are decoded once (not on the audio thread) to 32-bit float at their own rate and handed to the engine by pointer; a pad's swapped-out sample is freed two blocks later (as Kessel's sample voice).
- **Playback**: a read position in the sample's frames, advanced by (sample rate / host rate) × 2^(tune/12) × the sync ratio, read with 4-point cubic interpolation; reversed regions read backwards from END. Loops wrap within START … END with a 5 ms crossfade at the seam.
- **Per voice**: the envelope (linear attack, exponential decay), then the filter (`krate::Biquad`-style state-variable, cutoff swept down by the envelope), drive (tanh into a wave folder above 50 %), the bitcrusher (sample-and-hold rate reduction, then quantization), the ring modulator, the gate (if on), level and pan, sends.
- **Mix**: the dry pads, plus the delay and the reverb returns, then Volume.

## Starter kit

On first run (no `pads.json`) Klang writes 16 sounds it synthesizes into its library and puts them on the pads with their settings: kick, snare, closed and open hats (choke group 1), clank, pipe (ring-modulated), zap, boom, a one-bar break at 120 BPM (looped, SYNC 4), a two-bar drone (looped, gated, filtered), two vowel-like voices (one crushed), a reverse swell, a gated stab, a bass pluck on C2, a glitch. Patterns A (an industrial beat, the drone and stabs under a 3-against-4 gate) and B (the break with the voices) come with them. **STARTER KIT** on the panel puts them back.
