# Krate as a DAW: roadmap

Krate grows into a studio of its own: the rack of instruments it already is, plus recording, an arranger, editing, automation and export, instead of handing the instruments to another DAW as plugins. The instruments stay built in (their panels as they are), and the rack can later host CLAP plugins alongside them.

What Krate already has to build on: one transport (with JACK transport sync to other apps), the rack running every instrument with its panel, a mixer with EQ and sends, an 8-track pattern sequencer with a piano roll, MIDI and Guitar Pro song import, audio inputs (Krunch), sessions, rack-wide MIDI Learn, an offline renderer.

Each milestone ends with something you can use, and is committed and pushed when done.

| # | Milestone | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 ✅ | **Add and remove instruments** | The rack holds only the instruments you add (**+** to add and remove them), one of each; an instrument not in the rack has no tab, no mixer strip, no MIDI and costs no CPU, and is left out of the sequencer's and the song player's choices; the set is saved with the session. Every instrument is still built at startup (their panels are one per app), so adding and removing is instant; plugins (D7) come and go for real. Result: **+** after the tabs lists the instruments, each with ADD / REMOVE (the backing track always stays). In the rack, a device has a `present` flag (any thread): one not present isn't processed (its JACK outputs are fed silence), takes no MIDI (its MIDI channel falls back to the focused instrument), no focus (stepping, Program Change, learned focus buttons pass it by), no solo, feeds nothing (Krunch fed by it goes back to the interface's input), and its learned buttons don't fire; back in the rack, it's prepared afresh (no hanging notes). Its tab, panel (not even made) and mixer strip are hidden; the sequencer's and the song player's instrument menus leave it out, showing a track's choice "(not in rack)" if it's there already, while their lists stay whole so saved songs and sequences keep their choices; the song player doesn't suggest it. The session keeps the list (`"absent"`; older sessions: every instrument). Tests: tst_rack (skipped and silent, outputs cleared, no focus / solo / feed, reset on return), tst_racklearn (remove and add, focus moving, the backing track staying, the session), tst_song (suggestions only from the rack). |
| D2 ✅ | **Audio tracks and recording** | Audio tracks that record from an input (a guitar through Krunch, a microphone) or from any instrument's output to WAV files on disk, without dropouts (a disk thread fed from the audio thread through a lock-free buffer), and play back in time with the rack, the latency of the interface compensated. Result: eight tracks, **Audio 1** to **Audio 8**, added from **+** like an instrument (out of the rack until then; the rack now holds up to 32 devices and the host 66 output channels). Each is a rack device with a mixer strip and a panel: **ARM**, **MONITOR** (Off / Auto: while armed / On), **INPUT** (Stereo / In 1 / In 2, mono takes from one input), **SOURCE** (the interface's inputs, or any instrument's output in stereo: the mixer's input selector), an input meter, the active take's waveform and the list of takes (click one to play it, again for none; ✕ twice deletes its file). **REC** in the transport bar (learnable) arms recording: armed tracks record while the transport plays, a play from the top starting a new take. The audio thread copies the input into a lock-free ring (ten seconds); a disk thread writes it as 24-bit WAV to `~/.local/share/krate/recordings/audioN/take-<date>-<time>.wav`, and a new take waits until the last is written. A finished take is loaded off the GUI thread and becomes the track's active one; it plays at the transport's beat from where it was recorded, skipping the interface's round trip when it came from the interface (none from an instrument, which is already in time). The takes, each track's active take and its knobs are kept in `recordings/tracks.json`; quitting while recording keeps the take. Code: `shared/rec` (AudioRing, WavWriter, AudioTrack, Recorder; no Qt) and `apps/krate/AudioTracks` (the `Tracks` controller), `AudioTrackPanel.qml`. Tests: tst_recording (the ring, 24-bit files, recording and playing back in time, monitoring, mono, one take at a time, the disk thread), tst_audiotracks (a take through the controller kept, reloaded, picked and deleted; quitting mid-take; the registry entries). Checked live through PipeWire: Klick recorded into Audio 1 lands on every beat, sample-aligned from the take's start. |
| D3 ✅ | **The arranger** | A song timeline: per track, patterns, MIDI clips and audio clips laid out across bars, instead of one pattern looping; loop and punch ranges. Result: an **Arrange** screen (tab, `A`, learnable). Its lanes are the sequencer's 8 tracks (each already aimed at an instrument) and the audio tracks in the rack, across bars of 4 beats at the rack's one tempo. **SONG** plays the arrangement instead of the patterns. On a MIDI lane a click places the chosen pattern for its own length (drag for longer; it repeats, cut at the clip's end), a click on a clip opens its pattern in the Seq screen, a right click or **ERASE** removes it. **R** arms a lane: with SONG and **REC** on, what you play on the keyboard while the transport plays becomes a MIDI clip over the bars it spans, replacing the clips there (with LOOP each pass adds to the take; with PUNCH only notes inside the range count). Audio lanes show their takes (each track's takes now go on a lane, a later one on top; the track panel puts a take on or off it). The ruler sets where play starts; the **LOOP** and **PUNCH** strips are dragged to set those ranges. The playhead follows, the page turns with it. Start, loop and punch apply in SONG mode only (patterns play as before otherwise); while a DAW leads over JACK, its position and loop do. How: the transport starts at a beat and wraps at the loop's end with `restarted`, the rack processor splitting the block there so the wrap is on the exact sample; the sequencer's SONG mode plays an `Arrangement` (lanes of clips, MIDI clips' notes) at the transport's beat, ending held notes on a jump; audio tracks play a playlist of clips and punch in and out on the sample; the rack processor taps the keyboard's notes (with their beats) into a lock-free queue for the arranger. All of it is kept with the sequence (`sequencer.json` "arrange", saved sequences); audio clips in `recordings/tracks.json`. Tests: tst_transport (start, loop on the sample), tst_rack (the processor's split), tst_racksequencer (pattern and MIDI clips, a note across the wrap, play from a bar), tst_recording (clips on top of each other, punch mid-block), tst_audiotracks (lanes, older files), tst_sequencerfile, tst_seqcontroller (placing, SONG mode and the transport, recording MIDI clips, punch). Checked live through PipeWire: SONG from bar 5 with punch on bars 5-8 recorded Korrode into Audio 1 as one take at beat 16, exactly 4 bars, its notes on the step grid; MIDI sent into `krate:midi_in` became a clip with the notes' lengths and spacing to the tick. |
| D4 | **Clip editing** | Move, trim, split, duplicate and fade clips; undo and redo for every edit. |
| D5 | **Automation** | Knob movements recorded while playing, and drawn as lanes per track. |
| D6 | **Bounce and export** | The whole song (or a range, or each track) rendered to WAV, faster than real time. |
| D7 | **CLAP plugins in the rack** | Third-party CLAP instruments and effects added to the rack like the built-in ones, their windows opened from their tabs, their state saved with the session. |

Decisions:

- **Grow Krate rather than build plugins for another DAW, or fork one**: everything stays the way we want it, and the instruments' panels need no work (as plugins they would have to run as separate programs next to the host).
- **One of each instrument** for now: each instrument's controllers exist once per app. Copies (two Kobalts) can come later as their own project.
